# CityLab Diagnostics — Medical Glossary, Aliases, and Unit Conversions

Document ID: CLD-GLS-002
Authority: CityLab Diagnostics Reference Standards

## Section 1: Canonical Tests, Documented Aliases, and Plain-Language Glossary

| Test ID | Canonical Name | Documented Aliases | What It Measures (Class-8 Plain English) | Meaning of Low Value | Meaning of High Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| alt | ALT | SGPT; Alanine Aminotransferase; Alanine Transaminase | ALT is an enzyme mostly found inside liver cells that helps the body break down proteins for energy. | A value below the typical range is rarely a concern on its own, though it can occasionally be seen with low vitamin B6 or reduced muscle mass. | A higher-than-usual ALT level means extra enzyme has entered the bloodstream, which can happen when liver cells are stressed, inflamed, or working harder than usual. |
| ast | AST | SGOT; Aspartate Aminotransferase | AST is an enzyme found in the liver, heart, and muscles that helps process amino acids. | A slightly low AST value is generally not considered clinically harmful on its own. | An elevated AST value shows that cells in the liver or muscles may be irritated or strained and releasing extra enzyme into the blood. |
| fbs | FBS | Fasting Blood Sugar; Fasting Plasma Glucose; Fasting Glucose | FBS measures how much sugar (glucose) is in your blood after you have not eaten for at least 8 hours. | A low fasting blood sugar means your blood has less fuel available than usual, which can leave you feeling shaky, dizzy, or tired. | A high fasting blood sugar means more glucose is staying in your bloodstream than expected after fasting, so your body may need help moving sugar into your cells. |
| hba1c | HbA1c | Glycated Hemoglobin; Hemoglobin A1c | HbA1c shows your average blood sugar level over the past 2 to 3 months by measuring sugar attached to red blood cells. | A very low HbA1c usually points to lower average blood sugar levels over recent months or shorter red blood cell lifespan. | A high HbA1c indicates that average blood sugar levels have been running higher than typical over the past few months. |
| total_cholesterol | Total Cholesterol | Cholesterol, Total; Serum Cholesterol | Total Cholesterol adds up the different kinds of fat-like cholesterol particles circulating in your blood. | An unusually low total cholesterol level can occasionally be linked to poor nutrient absorption or an overactive metabolism. | A high total cholesterol level means there is extra cholesterol circulating in the blood, which doctors watch because it can slowly build up inside blood vessels over time. |
| hdl | HDL Cholesterol | HDL-C; High-Density Lipoprotein | HDL is often called helpful or "good" cholesterol because it carries extra cholesterol away from your blood vessels back to the liver. | A low HDL value means fewer cleanup particles are available to help clear excess cholesterol from the bloodstream. | A higher HDL value is generally seen as supportive for clearing extra cholesterol from blood vessels. |
| ldl | LDL Cholesterol | LDL-C; Low-Density Lipoprotein | LDL carries cholesterol from the liver out to cells, and excess amounts can collect along blood vessel walls. | A low LDL level generally means less cholesterol is being deposited along vessel walls, which is usually desirable unless extremely low. | A high LDL level means extra cholesterol is traveling in a form that can build up inside arteries over time. |
| triglycerides | Triglycerides | TG; Fasting Triglycerides | Triglycerides are a common type of blood fat that stores unused energy from the food you eat. | A low triglyceride level can reflect low body fat stores, fasting, or limited fat intake. | A high triglyceride level means extra blood fats are circulating, often influenced by diet, energy balance, or metabolism. |
| creatinine | Serum Creatinine | Creatinine; Blood Creatinine | Creatinine is a normal chemical waste made by everyday muscle use that healthy kidneys filter out into urine. | A low creatinine level can happen when someone has smaller muscle mass or low protein intake. | A high creatinine level suggests the kidneys may be filtering waste from the blood more slowly than usual, or that the body is dehydrated. |
| bun | Blood Urea Nitrogen | BUN; Urea Nitrogen | BUN measures urea nitrogen, a leftover waste from breaking down protein in food that your kidneys filter out. | A low BUN can occur with low protein intake or extra fluid in the body. | A high BUN can appear when the body is dehydrated, digesting a high-protein meal, or when the kidneys are clearing waste more slowly. |
| tsh | TSH | Thyroid Stimulating Hormone; Thyrotropin | TSH is a messenger hormone from a small gland in your brain that tells your thyroid gland how fast to run your body's energy speed. | A low TSH often means the brain is telling the thyroid to slow down because thyroid hormone levels are already high. | A high TSH usually means the brain is sending a stronger signal to ask a sluggish thyroid gland to make more hormone. |
| hemoglobin | Hemoglobin | Hb; Hgb | Hemoglobin is the iron-rich protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your body. | A low hemoglobin level means your blood carries less oxygen than usual, which can make you feel tired, weak, or short of breath. | A high hemoglobin level means there are more oxygen-carrying cells in the blood, which can happen from dehydration or living at high altitude. |
| wbc | WBC Count | WBC; White Blood Cell Count; Total Leukocyte Count; TLC | White blood cells are your body's defense team that helps fight germs and respond to irritation or swelling. | A low white blood cell count means fewer immune cells are circulating, which can temporarily lower your defense against infections. | A high white blood cell count usually means your immune system is actively responding to germs, stress, or inflammation. |
| platelets | Platelet Count | Platelets; PLT | Platelets are tiny cell pieces in the blood that stick together to help stop bleeding when you get a cut. | A low platelet count means the blood may take longer than normal to form clots and stop bruising or bleeding. | A high platelet count means extra clotting cell fragments are present in the blood, sometimes in response to inflammation or low iron. |
| potassium | Serum Potassium | Potassium; K+ | Potassium is an important mineral (electrolyte) that helps nerves send signals and keeps your heart and muscles beating steadily. | A low potassium level can disturb normal muscle and heart electrical signals, causing weakness or cramps. | A high potassium level can interfere with how the heart and muscles fire electrical signals and needs prompt medical review. |
| sodium | Serum Sodium | Sodium; Na+ | Sodium is a key salt mineral in blood that controls water balance and helps nerves and muscles work properly. | A low sodium level means there is too much water compared to salt in the blood, which can cause confusion or fatigue. | A high sodium level means the blood has more salt relative to water, often connected to dehydration or fluid loss. |
| urine_protein | Urine Protein | Urinary Protein; Protein, Urine Quantitative | Urine Protein checks whether protein—which normally stays inside the blood—is leaking out through the kidney filters into urine. | Low or minimal protein in urine is expected because healthy kidney filters keep most protein inside the blood. | Elevated protein in urine shows that the kidney filters may be letting more protein pass through than usual. |
